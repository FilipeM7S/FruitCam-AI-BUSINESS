## 1. Concepts in plain words

**Raster vs. vector.** A raster image (PNG, JPEG) is a grid of pixels; enlarging it makes it blurry. A vector image (SVG) is a list of shapes and is sharp at any size. The logo kit arrived as one raster image, so the logos here are raster crops. They are marked **interim** everywhere until the original vector files exist.

**Plate.** The supplied logos sit on a solid background colour (cream for the light version, dark green "ink" for the dark one). Instead of cutting the logo out (which would mean redrawing its edges), the app uses the logo *with* its background as a rectangle, a "plate", and paints the page in exactly the same colour. Plate and page then merge with no visible edge. That is why the app's light background is the brand cream `#f5f1e6` and the dark background is the brand ink `#14231e`.

**Clear space and minimum size.** Brand rules say how much empty space must surround the logo (here 0.25 × the height of the drawing) and how small it may get (20 px for the full logo, 16 px for the icon). Both numbers live in exactly one file, [brand/rules.json](brand/rules.json). Everything else reads them from there.

**Responsive images.** A phone does not need a 1920 px photo. The page lists several widths and formats (`srcset`), states how wide the image will be on screen (`sizes`), and the browser picks the smallest file that looks sharp. **AVIF** and **WebP** are modern formats; here AVIF files are 35–75% smaller than the JPEG of the same photo and width. JPEG stays as the fallback. Every `<img>` has `width` and `height`, so the page reserves the space before the image arrives and nothing jumps.

**Lazy loading.** Images and the video below the visible area are not downloaded until the user scrolls near them.

**Licences.** A photo may be used only if its licence allows it. **CC0** means "no conditions". **CC BY** means "credit the author". **CC BY-SA** means "credit the author, and adaptations must keep the same licence". Every third-party photo here comes from Wikimedia Commons, where licence and author are recorded per file. The app credits each one visibly and lists all of them on the *Créditos* page.

**Illustrative.** These photos are not from our operation: they are not our field, our factory or our customers, and they are not outputs of our model. Each one carries an "Imagem ilustrativa" badge.

**Synthetic vs. real data.** Synthetic data is generated by code with a known answer. It is perfect for showing *how* a statistic works and for testing it, but it says nothing about the real world. Every figure says in its corner which kind it is.

**Confidence intervals and prediction intervals.** A proportion measured on 50 fruits is uncertain. The 95% Wilson interval is the range that would contain the true proportion in 95% of repeated samples. A prediction interval does the same for *next* week's value, and it is wider because it also carries next week's own randomness.

**Calibration.** If a model says "90% sure" a thousand times, it should be right about 900 times. A reliability diagram plots *stated* confidence against *observed* accuracy. **ECE** (expected calibration error) is the average gap between the two.

**Data leakage.** If the same source photo yields crops in both the training and the validation set, validation looks easier than real life. Here 59% of the validation images come from photos that also have crops in training (measured, Figure 6).

**Grad-CAM.** A way to colour the parts of the input that pushed the model toward its answer, computed from gradients inside the network. It shows *where* the gradient concentrates; it does not prove *why* the model decided.

**Motion with transform and opacity only.** Browsers can animate `transform` (move, scale, rotate) and `opacity` on the graphics card without recomputing layout. Animating anything else (width, colour, top/left) forces work on every frame and stutters on cheap phones. A test enforces this rule over the whole stylesheet.

**Reduced motion.** People with vestibular disorders can ask the operating system to reduce motion. The app then shows everything in its final state: no animation, no autoplaying video.

**CSP.** A Content-Security-Policy tells the browser "only run scripts and load files from this server". Injected scripts, inline styles and third-party embeds are refused. This app runs with the strictest useful policy: no inline scripts, no `eval`, no external origins.

**Compression and caching.** Text files (JS, CSS, SVG) shrink by 60–80% with Brotli. They are compressed once at build time and served pre-compressed. Files whose name contains a content hash (`index-FRWZoh30.js`, `fig1-pipeline.299ef96ff4.svg`) can be cached "forever" (`immutable`), because a changed file gets a new name.

## 2. Where things come from: the asset pipeline

Everything you see is generated by scripts. Nothing is edited by hand, so everything can be rebuilt and checked.

```
brand/source/logo-kit-sheet.png ─┐
brand/rules.json ────────────────┴─ tools/build_brand.py ──► frontend/public/brand/*, manifest.webmanifest, src/brand.json

Wikimedia Commons API ─ tools/fetch_media.py ──► media_src/commons/*.jpg + media_src/sources.json
media_src/catalog.json ─┐
media_src/video/*  ─────┼─ tools/build_media.py ──► frontend/public/media/*, src/media.json, src/licenses.json
src/figures.json ───────┘

model.pt + data_real/val ─ figures/evaluate_model.py ──► figures/data/model_eval.json
figures/captions.json ──── figures/make_figures.py ───► figures/out/*.svg|png, frontend/public/figures/*, src/figures.json

frontend/dist (built app) ─ tools/record_demo.py ─────► media_src/video/* (demo, pipeline clip, storyboard, screenshots)

all of the above ────────── tools/build_summit_pack.py ► summit_pack/
```

The JSON files in `frontend/src/` (`brand.json`, `media.json`, `figures.json`, `licenses.json`) are the contract between the generators and the Vue app. The app never hard-codes a file name that a script produces.

## 3. Brand and logos

### Decisions

- **No redrawing.** The logos are exact pixel crops of the supplied sheet ([brand/source/logo-kit-sheet.png](brand/source/logo-kit-sheet.png), byte-identical copy). Colours, proportions and letterforms are untouched. Downscaled renditions are made with high-quality resampling and lossless WebP. A test proves the plate border is still *exactly* the page colour.
- **Plates, not cut-outs.** The light-background and dark-background lockups are used as plates. The app's backgrounds are set to the plate colours, so the plates disappear into the page.
- **The app icon is the one exception**: it has rounded corners and must sit on any background. Its corners were made transparent by exact two-colour unmixing (each corner pixel is a mix of the beige outside and the dark tile; the mix ratio becomes the transparency). The logo mark itself is never touched. The builder asserts that.
- **Theme-based variant selection.** [BrandLogo.vue](frontend/src/components/BrandLogo.vue) picks the light or dark plate from the current theme (or a forced surface, such as the always-dark footer). It then picks the smallest rendition that is at least 2× the displayed height (sharp on retina screens), and it never renders below the minimum size from `rules.json`.

### Code

- [brand/rules.json](brand/rules.json): single source for clear space (0.25), minimum ink heights (20 px lockup, 16 px icon), and where each logo sits on the sheet.
- [tools/build_brand.py](tools/build_brand.py)
  - `plate()` crops each lockup with clear space and asserts the outer border is pure background. `ink_box()` then finds the *visible* ink (pixels more than 24/255 away from the background) and asserts the margins on all four sides are at least 0.25 × the ink height. The measured ink heights are 179 px (light) and 97 px (dark); the hand-entered regions said 186 and 100, so the code uses the measured, smaller and more honest values.
  - `icon()` unmixes the corners (described above) and asserts the mark stays out of the edge band.
  - `lossless()` writes WebP and re-reads it, asserting the pixels are identical to the source.
  - Outputs: `logo-on-light/dark.png` (originals), `.webp` copies, renditions `-h80`/`-h192`, the app icon, favicons (`favicon.ico` 16/32/48 px, `favicon-32.png`), PWA icons (192, 512, 512 maskable), the Apple touch icon, the social preview `og-image.png` (1200×630), `manifest.webmanifest` and [frontend/src/brand.json](frontend/src/brand.json).
- Placement in the app:
  - **loading**: the boot splash in [frontend/index.html](frontend/index.html). It shows before any JavaScript runs, and CSS picks the light or dark plate.
  - **top bar and footer**: [App.vue](frontend/src/App.vue). The footer is always dark.
  - **login**: [LoginView.vue](frontend/src/views/LoginView.vue), with an animated reveal.
  - **empty state**: [DashboardView.vue](frontend/src/views/DashboardView.vue), the icon next to the "no real data" notice.
  - **favicon, PWA, social preview**: `index.html` and the manifest. `/favicon.ico` redirects to the real file ([fruitcam/urls.py](fruitcam/urls.py)). The `og:image` URL is made absolute per request by `spa()` in [core/views.py](core/views.py), because link-preview bots need a full URL.
- **Animated reveal with static fallback**: `logo-in` keyframes (opacity + transform). Under reduced motion all animations are off, so the logo is simply there.
- **Theme without flash**: [frontend/public/theme.js](frontend/public/theme.js) is a 221-byte script in `<head>` that applies the saved theme *before* the page paints. It is an external file, because the CSP forbids inline scripts. The theme toggle (top bar and footer) lives in [media.js](frontend/src/media.js).

- [tools/build_media.py](tools/build_media.py): makes AVIF/WebP/JPEG at each width (never upscaling), with per-format byte budgets that lower quality until the file fits. File names carry a content hash. Writes:
  - [frontend/src/media.json](frontend/src/media.json): per variant, the alt text, the `illustrative` flag, a credit line, intrinsic size, `srcset` lists and the fallback.

Per photo: file, source URL, author, licence + URL, date, retrieval date, full attribution text, the modifications made, where it is used (app pages, figures, video, screenshots) and every derived file. First-party entries cover the brand files, the figures and the demo video.
- [ResponsiveImage.vue](frontend/src/components/ResponsiveImage.vue): `<picture>` with AVIF → WebP → JPEG, `width`/`height`, `loading="lazy"` (or eager + `fetchpriority="high"` for the login hero and the banner), and a visible caption: an "Imagem ilustrativa" badge plus a credit link to its entry on the *Créditos* page. Where several small images share a block (fruit cards, photo guide), one badge plus one credit per photo appears under the block.
- [CreditsView.vue](frontend/src/views/CreditsView.vue): renders `licenses.json`, with anchors per photo so each credit link jumps to its entry.

### Honesty choices visible in the UI

- The fruit cards for castanha and melão say "Sem fotos de treino: resultado não validado". The model was trained on cashew only.
- The photo guide says its advice follows from how the current pipeline works (whole-photo classifier + threshold box) and is not validated in the field.

## 5. Scientific figures

Six figures, generated by [figures/make_figures.py](figures/make_figures.py) in a journal style: 183 mm wide (double column), DejaVu Sans, Okabe-Ito colour-blind-safe palette, labelled axes with units, `n` on every data panel, uncertainty on every estimate, bold panel letters, pt-BR number formatting (decimal comma, true minus sign). Each figure is exported as **SVG** (vector, text as paths so it looks identical everywhere) and **PNG at 300 dpi**. The bottom-right corner of each figure carries a data-source label, embedded as an SVG element with a fixed id (`synthetic-label`, `real-label`, `schematic-label`, `example-label`). The test reads that id back.

| # | Title | Data | What it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Do clique ao painel | schematic | the 7 steps, which one is learned (only the classifier), what passes between steps |
| 2 | O que o classificador recebe e devolve | real examples, not a metric | a CC0 cashew photo with the Otsu box (which fails with foliage), the 224×224 input with Grad-CAM from the real model, real probabilities, and a synthetic ellipse that also gets 99.8% confidence ("confidence is not correctness") |
| 3 | Como um evento vira estatística | synthetic | event rows → weekly counts with n → weekly proportion with Wilson 95% CIs |
| 4 | Previsão e incerteza | synthetic | linear trend with a 95% prediction interval vs. the true next value; a short, small series gets a wide interval and is flagged unreliable; rolling-origin backtest coverage over 20 series with the binomial band expected at 95% |
| 5 | Ajuste gaussiano e teste de normalidade | synthetic | histogram + fitted normal with ±1σ/±2σ, a Q-Q plot with a 2000-simulation envelope, Shapiro-Wilk; a skewed sector whose fitted normal spills into impossible negative proportions |
| 6 | Qualidade do modelo no conjunto de validação | **real measurement** | confusion matrices (all validation; only photos with no crops in training), per-class sensitivity and accuracy with Wilson CIs against the "always rotten" baseline, a reliability diagram with ECE |

Model-explanation visuals come **only** from the real `model.pt`. The confusion matrices and the reliability diagram are computed on the validation images by [figures/evaluate_model.py](figures/evaluate_model.py). The Grad-CAM example (`model_view()` in `make_figures.py`) uses the CC0 Commons photo, because the validation images cannot be published (unknown licence). That photo is not one of the project's dataset files; whether a similar picture was among the training crops' unknown sources cannot be checked. The evaluation's findings, stated on the "Como funciona" page and in the captions:

- Validation n = 516: accuracy 91.7% (95% CI 89.0–93.8%); always answering "podre" would score 78.1%; balanced accuracy 88.9%; ECE 0.066.
- 59% of validation images come from photos that also have crops in training, and this set was used to pick the checkpoint. It is **not** an independent test.
- On the 212 images whose source photos have no crops in training: accuracy 95.8% vs. 96.2% for "always rotten", with only 8 good fruits. There is no evidence of beating the baseline there.
- Field performance: **not evaluated**. The page shows "Ainda não avaliado" cards for that and for the other open items.

Layout details: panel letters are annotations placed beside left-aligned titles, so they cannot overlap; a band at the bottom is reserved for the data label; Figure 2 is 2×2 so the photos stay legible. On the web, vector figures are served as SVG and Figure 2 (mostly photos) as a 1600 px WebP (99 KB instead of a 743 KB SVG with an embedded photo). The SVG and PNG remain available for download. Below 640 px wide each figure scrolls sideways inside its own box (keyboard-focusable, with a hint) instead of shrinking into illegible text.

Captions, alt texts and labels live in [figures/captions.json](figures/captions.json). `publish()` copies hashed files to `frontend/public/figures/` and writes [frontend/src/figures.json](frontend/src/figures.json) (including the measured numbers the page quotes).

## 6. Demo video, pipeline clip and storyboard

[tools/record_demo.py](tools/record_demo.py) records the **real app running**:

1. Starts the built app on a throwaway SQLite database. It seeds 40 weeks of synthetic demo data with Portuguese sector names, then warms the server up (first model load, first statistics) and deletes the warm-up record, so the clip shows a running server, not a cold start. Nothing in the footage is sped up or cut.
2. Drives Edge through Playwright: login → choose "Caju" and sector → upload the CC0 cashew photo → scanning line → result → dashboard with demo data → scroll through charts.
3. Captures frames with the Chrome DevTools screencast, at real timestamps, and encodes with ffmpeg (bundled by `imageio-ffmpeg`): **WebM/VP9** (0.82 MB) and **MP4/H.264** (1.31 MB), 1280×720, 26.5 s, 30 fps, **no audio track**.
4. Writes WebVTT captions (pt-BR, 6 cues) and the matching transcript from the same step markers, so they cannot drift apart. It also writes a poster frame and four storyboard stills. While recording, it logs which third-party photos are on screen at each step. That list feeds the licence manifest, so the video's attribution is exact (the login photo and the photo-guide thumbnails appear in it too).
5. Records the pipeline animation from "Como funciona" as MP4 + GIF for slides.
6. Takes two 2× screenshots (result screen, dashboard) for the Summit pack.

The model's answer shown in the video (Boa, 99.8%) is its real output for that photo. The captions say the box covers the whole photo because Otsu thresholding fails on a leafy background, and that the dashboard's defect types are simulated (the model only distinguishes good and rotten).

In the app, [LoopVideo.vue](frontend/src/components/LoopVideo.vue):
- `muted loop playsinline preload="none"`; WebM first, MP4 fallback; a `<track kind="captions" srclang="pt-BR" default>`.
- Plays only while at least 35% visible and pauses when scrolled away (IntersectionObserver). The poster is attached only when the video comes within 600 px of the viewport, so the page doesn't pay for it up front.
- Under reduced motion or a constrained connection (Save-Data, 2G/3G), no `<video>` element is created at all: you get the poster as a responsive image and a "Reproduzir vídeo" button.
- A play/pause button with `aria-pressed`, a "Gravação do app · dados sintéticos" badge, the caption, and a collapsible transcript.

## 7. The app: components, pages and motion

### Theme and surfaces ([style.css](frontend/src/style.css))

- Colour tokens are defined once. Light uses cream `#f5f1e6`, dark uses ink `#14231e`, switched by the OS setting or by `data-theme` on `<html>`.
- "Paper" surfaces (cards, panels, the login card, filters) keep the light tokens in both themes. Charts, forms and figures stay on white, legible paper while the page around them goes dark. That's why the dashboard charts needed no dark re-theming.
- The top bar is opaque and uses the page colour, so the logo plate in it is seamless.

### Pages

- **Login** ([LoginView.vue](frontend/src/views/LoginView.vue)): hero photo (half the screen on desktop, a strip on mobile), logo reveal, tagline, the form, and a link to "Como funciona".
- **Analyze** ([UploadView.vue](frontend/src/views/UploadView.vue), presentation only; upload logic untouched): photo fruit cards, a photo guide ("Assim" / "Evite") in the empty result panel, a scanning line over the preview while inference runs, a shaped skeleton of the result in the result panel, then boxes drawn one by one (staggered `box-in`), the verdict "pops", and the confidence bar fills (`meter-in`).
- **Como funciona** ([HowItWorksView.vue](frontend/src/views/HowItWorksView.vue)): banner; the **step-by-step pipeline animation** ([PipelineAnimation.vue](frontend/src/components/PipelineAnimation.vue)), which plays when scrolled into view, announces each step in an `aria-live` line, and has a replay button; the six figures; what was measured and on which set; "Ainda não avaliado" items; the demo video; what was verified and what was not.
- **Créditos** ([CreditsView.vue](frontend/src/views/CreditsView.vue)).
- **Dashboard**: the brand icon in the "no real data" empty state; the fruit chart got more axis headroom so its callout no longer clips ([charts.js](frontend/src/charts.js), presentation only).

### Motion rules

- Every keyframe and transition animates only `transform`/`opacity` (enforced by `frontend/tests/series.test.mjs`).
- JavaScript animation uses the Web Animations API (`element.animate`) with `fill: "backwards"`, so finished animations release the element back to CSS.
- Scroll reveals: the `v-reveal` directive ([media.js](frontend/src/media.js)) adds a class when a block enters the viewport. Under reduced motion (and when printing) blocks are visible immediately.
- Page transitions (`route-*`), micro-interactions (fruit-card lift, theme button rotate, button press), shaped skeletons and the boot-splash fade.
- **Reduced motion is fully static**: `animation: none` on everything, transitions shortened to effectively zero (kept non-zero so "transition finished" events still fire), the pipeline is drawn complete, there's no video element, and reveals are shown at once. Tested by counting running animations on every page and by comparing screenshots taken 0.7 s apart.
- Code splitting ([router.js](frontend/src/router.js)): Chart.js (67 KB compressed) loads only on the dashboard; "Como funciona" and "Créditos" load on demand.

## 8. Server: CSP, compression, caching

[fruitcam/settings.py](fruitcam/settings.py):

- Django 6's built-in `ContentSecurityPolicyMiddleware`, with: `default-src 'self'; script-src 'self'; style-src 'self'; img-src 'self' data: blob:; media-src 'self'; font-src 'self'; connect-src 'self'; manifest-src 'self'; worker-src 'none'; object-src 'none'; base-uri 'self'; form-action 'self'; frame-ancestors 'none'`. Nothing `unsafe-*`. Vue's style bindings and the Web Animations API are not blocked by this policy; inline `<script>`/`<style>` and `eval` are.
- `WHITENOISE_IMMUTABLE_FILE_TEST`: built assets and every hash-named file in `media/` and `figures/` get `Cache-Control: max-age=315360000, immutable`. Un-hashed files (brand, manifest, `theme.js`) keep the short default.
- `WHITENOISE_MIMETYPES`: `.vtt` → `text/vtt` and `.webmanifest` → `application/manifest+json`. Without this WhiteNoise sent `application/octet-stream`, and with `nosniff` the browser would refuse the captions track. Found by checking WhiteNoise's type table; the delivery test now asserts every type.
- [frontend/scripts/compress.mjs](frontend/scripts/compress.mjs) runs after `vite build` and writes `.br` (Brotli 11) and `.gz` (gzip 9) next to every text file of 1 KB or more. WhiteNoise serves the best one the browser accepts. Before this change, JavaScript was served **uncompressed**.

A side effect of the CSP: Playwright's `wait_for_function` uses `eval`, which the policy now blocks, so the existing browser test helper in [core/test_dashboard.py](core/test_dashboard.py) now polls with `page.evaluate` instead.

## 14. What was not verified

- **Original logo files.** Not supplied. The logos are interim raster crops of the kit image; they must be replaced by the vector originals (`rules.json` lists the expected names). Edge quality at very large sizes (posters) is limited by the source resolution: the light lockup is 678×278 px.
- **Browsers.** Tests run on Microsoft Edge (Chromium) on Windows only. Safari/iOS and Firefox were not tested, so neither was the MP4 fallback path (the file is served correctly over HTTP, but no browser played it in a test).
- **Assistive technology.** Alt texts, labels, `aria-live`, captions and focusable regions are checked structurally. No screen reader (NVDA, VoiceOver, TalkBack) session was run.
- **Real networks and devices.** Page weight was measured on localhost. Load times on 3G and on low-end phones were not measured. The Save-Data / slow-connection fallback for video depends on `navigator.connection`, which Safari and Firefox don't expose; it was not exercised in a test.
- **Social previews and PWA.** The `og:image` URL is made absolute per request, but no real platform (WhatsApp, LinkedIn, Slack) was asked to render a preview. PWA install and the maskable icon on Android launchers were not checked on a device.
- **Licensing review.** Licences and authors are taken from Wikimedia Commons metadata as published; the uploaders' rights were not independently verified. CC BY-SA thumbnails appear inside the demo video and screenshots. They are attributed with exact placement, but no legal review was done of whether those works count as adaptations.
- **Model claims.** Figure 6 and the "Como funciona" numbers come from a validation set that was used to choose the checkpoint and shares source photos with training (59%), with unknown image provenance and licence. There is no independent test set, no field evaluation, and no data for castanha or melão. The Grad-CAM map is one example image; there is no batch saliency study.
- **Dark theme charts.** By design, cards stay light in dark mode. A fully dark chart theme was not built.
- **Print.** A print stylesheet hides chrome and shows hidden-for-animation content, but printing was not checked visually.
- **Layout metrics.** Images always carry dimensions (tested), but Lighthouse/CLS/LCP were not measured.
- **Dashboard latency (found, not fixed).** On the development server, the seven statistics requests run in parallel take 4–6 s on 47k synthetic events, because pandas work contends for Python's GIL; the same requests take about 2.4 s in sequence. This is outside this task (analytics untouched). Caching per filter or fewer, combined requests would address it. The demo clip uses a smaller synthetic dataset (14k events) rather than cutting the wait out.
